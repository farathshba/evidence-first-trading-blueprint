# Trading Lessons: The Beginner Path to Profitability

> Owner-authored lessons for traders who are unprofitable or breakeven. These are the things I wasted time NOT doing when starting out — what to abide by, and what to avoid, to actually become profitable and stay that way.

## 1. Do NOT join trade signal groups

Never take live trades because a signal group told you to take this trade or that trade. If you are already in one, only ever use their signals for **learning**: watch how the trade played out in the live market, understand the rationale behind it — and that's it. Never place live orders just because a signal community alerted you to.

## 2. Only trade a strategy in live markets after extensive backtesting

Know every single thing related to your strategy — all the confluences that laid its foundation. And even then, a successfully backtested strategy can still fail live purely because of **your psychology**:

- You cut your stop-losses shorter, or space them out randomly in some trades
- You exit the trade way before it actually hit your take-profit
- You skip confluences your strategy demands simply because you were bored and wanted to trade

Additional rules within this pointer:

- **Do NOT copy-paste a YouTube guru's strategy.** If many people know an edge, the edge doesn't exist for long. Learn the basics and intermediate concepts behind their strategy, then backtest with different confluences and find out what actually works.
- **A strategy only works on one specified instrument and one session.** A strategy that does phenomenally well in the Asian session might not be as good in the New York session, and vice versa. One that works amazingly on ETH will most probably be shit on Gold — and the other way around.

## 3. Treat this as an actual profession, not a side hustle

Every dollar you spend must be accounted for. No profitable trader ever got there without a pre-defined plan and a system to abide by — and, most importantly, they took trading as an actual profession, not some random side hustle to make some money and then get off the markets.

## 4. Stay curious — ask WHY

If you are just starting out, have the curiosity to ask why. Many people treat the market as a facade full of mysteries and unexpected moves. That is not entirely true — a decent number of market moves can be explained.

- Use Reuters and Bloomberg for news
- To get accurate explanations for why big moves occurred, use Pensor, MarketReader, or any AI model you're accustomed to — but never ignore such moves. Always try to know the reasoning behind them. That curiosity is what makes you fall in love with the market.

## 5. Never borrow money to trade

Do not EVER borrow money from anyone. Only trade with money you can actually afford to lose. Overly leveraged positions, borrowing from family to make that one big trade — that won't get you anywhere. Trust me on that.

## 6. Manage your risk

- If your total account size is **more than 5k**: never risk more than **0.5%** of the account on a trade
- If it is **less than 5k**: never risk more than **1%**
- I would suggest not starting with less than 5k of trading capital if you wish to trade your own live account. Prop-firm challenge accounts are an alternative if you can't afford 5k, but a live account is actually better — most prop firms are disguised money stealers. Your call.

## 7. Do NOT give up

You will feel like giving up almost every other day, especially when starting out:

- **Information overload** — so much news and data that you'll want to avoid it altogether
- **Losing streaks ruin your psychology** — this is honestly inevitable; nothing on earth can avoid a losing streak for a trader

Build the resilience to overcome it and stay in the game. Trading is not a race track — it is a **marathon**. You don't have to be fast; you just must NOT give up no matter what happens — and eventually you will WIN.

---

**One last thing:** DO NOT LET ONE LOSS RUIN YOUR CONFIDENCE, AND DO NOT LET ONE WIN BUILD YOUR EGO.
